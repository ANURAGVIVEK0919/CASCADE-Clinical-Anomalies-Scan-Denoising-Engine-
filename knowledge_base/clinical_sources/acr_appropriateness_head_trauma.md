## Section 1: Acute Head Trauma Imaging Indications
Noncontrast Computed Tomography (NCCT) of the head is the primary initial imaging modality of choice in acute moderate to severe closed head injury. Clinical decision rules (such as the Canadian CT Head Rule and the New Orleans Criteria) establish high-sensitivity criteria for identifying patients with mild TBI who require NCCT:
- GCS score < 15 at 2 hours post-injury.
- Suspected open or depressed skull fracture.
- Any sign of basal skull fracture (e.g., hemotympanum, raccoon eyes, Battle sign, CSF leak).
- Two or more episodes of vomiting.
- Age 65 years or older.
- Retrograde amnesia for events immediately preceding injury (>30 minutes).
- "Dangerous" mechanism of injury (e.g., pedestrian struck by motor vehicle, occupant ejected from motor vehicle, fall from elevation >= 3 feet or 5 stairs).

## Section 2: Acute Intracranial Hemorrhage Subtypes
NCCT allows rapid detection and characterization of acute hemorrhage patterns:
1. **Epidural Hematoma (EDH)**:
   - Typically biconvex (lenticular) high-attenuation extra-axial collection.
   - Usually limited by cranial sutures where periosteum adheres to the inner table of the skull.
   - Frequently associated with skull fractures crossing middle meningeal artery branches.
2. **Subdural Hematoma (SDH)**:
   - Typically crescent-shaped (concave-convex) extra-axial collection extending across suture lines.
   - Bound by dural reflections (falx cerebri, tentorium cerebelli).
   - Acute SDH typically exhibits hyperdense attenuation (50-70 Hounsfield Units).
3. **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH)**:
   - High attenuation within the basal cisterns, sulci, and sylvian fissures.
   - In trauma, most frequently localized along convexities adjacent to cortical contusions.
4. **Intraparenchymal Contusion & Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI)**:
   - Hemorrhagic punctate lesions commonly located in the inferior frontal and anterior temporal lobes.
   - DAI lesions typically occur at the gray-white matter junction, corpus callosum, and upper brainstem.
